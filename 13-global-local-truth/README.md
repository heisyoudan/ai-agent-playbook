# 13 — Global Truth, Local Truth & Truth Projection

[日本語](README.ja.md) · **English** · [简体中文](README.zh-CN.md)

**Status: Draft (chapter shell).** Maturity: **Working Hypothesis** — a plausible model, not yet supported by enough evidence to be treated as methodology. Tracked in the [Research Incubator](../research/README.md).

## The Question This Chapter Will Address

When a project holds more truth than any single agent can reason about, what does one subsystem actually need to know?

## Why This Chapter Exists

Giving every agent the entire project truth is not possible. Giving it a careless summary is worse than giving it nothing, because a plausible partial view is indistinguishable from a complete one.

**Projection** is the deliberate act of deciding which subset of global truth a given scope receives, and on what terms. The idea is attractive and partly observed. It also makes strong assumptions — that global truth is maintained well enough to project from, that a projection can be kept current, and that local reasoning from a projection will not silently contradict the whole. Those assumptions are the open question.

## Concepts Expected to Belong Here

- Global truth and local truth as distinct, differently owned things
- Projection as a designed view with an owner, not a filtered copy
- Consistency guarantees, and honestly stating where they do not hold
- Staleness windows: how long a projection may lag before it misleads
- What happens when local truth conflicts with global truth
- Projection contracts and invalidation triggers

## Current Maturity

Working hypothesis. Related to Chapter 08 (Truth Governance) and Chapter 09 (Effective Reasoning Scope), but the mechanism is untested at scale. Counterexamples are actively sought.
